# Muay Thai Gym and Coaching digital funnel #

## Introduction ##

I'm partnering with a Muay Thai gym in Phuket, Thailand (https://mjfivepointsmuaythaigym.com/) which was founded by quite a serious coach / trainer who has worked with former world-class fighters. He has a team of experienced trainers to help. 

What I want to do is create a funnel for people around the world to book. Our best guess at this stage is that customers will be mostly male, aged 18-35 years old and have an interest in Muay Thai. We are aiming the funnel at people outside and inside of Thailand. We have no idea what that split would be. Basically, the website could be improved in terms of content and SEO but the gym itself has a lot going for it. 

In addition to there being lots of local competition selling luxury resorts, the gym itself is a bit dilapidated so I thought of using the "Authentic", "No-frills", "Old-school", "Come here to work" rather than something to do on holidays. 

What I need is a funnel to attract attention via social media or other specialist websites for Muay Thai fighters which will encourage users to formally contact the gym via a digital channel and then I will get (maybe, TBD) 10% of each booking. There is a similar effort at https://www.fightescapes.com/ but this seems highly priced and only runs once a year. The person running this will only pay a small fraction (> 50%) to the gym itself. 

We intend the gym to handle payments to avoid financial responsibility but need to record each contact that a potential customer makes so that we can calculate what we have earned. 

I have a rough information architecture sketched out along with a wireframe of the home page.

## Revenue Attribution Strategy ##

### The Challenge
You need an auditable way to track which bookings originated from your funnel, especially since the gym handles payments directly.

### Recommended Approach: Lightweight CRM Tracking

**System:**
1. **Capture every inquiry** via your website form with: name, email, phone, inquiry date, package interested, referral source
2. **Log all contacts** in a simple tracking system (Google Sheet, Airtable, or HubSpot free tier)
3. **Monthly reconciliation** with gym: share inquiry list, cross-reference with their bookings
4. **Create a unique inquiry reference** (e.g., "MJ-20260522-001") that customer includes when booking

**Advantages:**
- Audit trail for disputes
- Gym sees legitimate demand sourcing
- Monthly reporting shows ROI
- Fair for both parties

**Alternative Tracking Methods:**

| Method | Pros | Cons |
|--------|------|------|
| Unique referral code per inquiry | Simple, trackable | Requires gym to log code; manual; error-prone |
| Dedicated email address | Easy sorting | Not modern; splits communication flow |
| Form submissions + CRM sync | Most reliable; best audit trail | Requires integration setup |
| Your own booking widget | Highest control | Gym loses direct control; adds friction |

### Next Step
Agree with gym on: "When a customer books, they'll reference the inquiry ID or say 'I contacted you via [your website]' so we can verify the referral."

## Marketing Channels ##

### Channel Priorities (In Recommended Order)

#### **Phase 1: Organic Content (Weeks 1-4)**
*Goal: Build credibility, test messaging, establish assets*

1. **YouTube Shorts / TikTok / Instagram Reels**
   - Short-form training clips (15-30 sec): Master Jud pad work, student transformations, gym atmosphere
   - Video titles: "Muay Thai Training in Phuket," "What Real Boxing Camp Looks Like," "No-Frills Muay Thai"
   - Post 2-3x/week; repurpose same footage across platforms
   - Why: High organic reach in Muay Thai niche; algorithm favors short video
   - Cost: Time only (use phone camera)

2. **Reddit Communities**
   - Post in: r/Muaythai, r/Thailand, r/MartialArts, r/FitnessGaming
   - Tone: Helpful advice, not salesy. "I train at a real gym in Phuket, happy to answer questions"
   - Share: Student stories, training breakdowns, logistics guidance
   - Why: High-trust audience; Muay Thai enthusiasts research camps obsessively
   - Cost: Time only

3. **Muay Thai Specialist Forums & Communities**
   - TrainMuay, Muay Thai Guys forum, Sherdog Muay Thai section
   - Guest posts or sponsorship: "Best Training Camps in Phuket," "Authentic vs. Resort Training"
   - Why: Hyperlocal audience, serious practitioners
   - Cost: Time + possibly small sponsorship ($100-300)

#### **Phase 2: Partnerships (Weeks 4-8)**
*Goal: Leverage influencers and existing audiences*

4. **Micro-Influencer Partnerships (5K-50K followers)**
   - Target: Muay Thai coaches, fighters, fitness YouTubers in the niche
   - Offer: Free or discounted week in exchange for honest review content
   - Why: 1-3 videos from credible voices = $1-5K worth of paid ads
   - Cost: 1-2 free weeks (~£600-1,200 in value per influencer)

5. **Instagram Influencer Outreach**
   - Look for: Muay Thai equipment sellers, fitness coaches, travel influencers targeting Southeast Asia
   - Offer: Affiliate arrangement (commission on bookings they send)
   - Why: Passive referral income for them; minimal cost to you
   - Cost: Commission-only (10% of what they generate)

#### **Phase 3: Paid Ads (Weeks 8+)**
*Only after you've proven the funnel converts*

6. **Google Ads**
   - Keywords: "muay thai training phuket," "muay thai camp," "training in thailand"
   - Bid: Low initially (~$1-2 per click); scale if CPA works
   - Why: Intent-based; people actively searching
   - Cost: $300-500/month to test

7. **Facebook/Instagram Ads**
   - Target: 18-35 males interested in Muay Thai, fitness, travel; lookalike audiences
   - Creative: Student testimonials + training footage
   - Cost: $300-500/month to test

### Channels NOT to Start With
- ❌ TikTok influencer sponsorships (too expensive, hard to track ROI)
- ❌ YouTube ads (expensive before PMF; use organic Shorts first)
- ❌ Cold email outreach (spammy, low conversion)
- ❌ Paid Facebook ads (until you prove organic funnel works)

### Success Metrics to Track
- Video views → click-through rate to website
- Website visitors → inquiry form completions
- Inquiries → bookings converted (your funnel's most important ratio)
- Cost per acquisition (CPa) = total marketing spend ÷ bookings converted

Before scaling, you need answers to these capacity & conversion questions:

8. **Will they follow up with "referred by [your funnel]" in their booking notes?**

### Logistics & Sustainability
9. **Do they have any blackout periods beyond Sundays?** (Thai holidays, renovations, etc.)
10. **What's the minimum viable booking rate for this partnership to work for them?**
11. **Are they willing to share monthly booking data so you can verify commissions?**

## Assumptions & Risks ##

### Assumptions Made
- Gym can absorb 20-40 students/month without quality degradation
- Online inquiries convert at ~20% (industry average for specialty services)
- £60-90 commission is sufficient to cover customer acquisition costs
- Master Jud & gym want to grow overseas enrollment

### Key Risks
1. **Low conversion rate** — If inquiry → booking rate is <10%, CAC may exceed commission value
2. **Gym capacity constraints** — If they're consistently full, your referrals sit in queue
3. **Payment/attribution disputes** — Without clear tracking, gym may not honor commissions
4. **Seasonal demand** — Nov-Feb peak; June-Sept trough may require different strategies
5. **Competitive pressure** — FightEscapes and other agencies may undercut pricing

### Mitigation Strategies
- Start with organic channels (low cost to test hypothesis)
- Establish tracking system **before** scaling paid ads
- Regular communication with gym (weekly check-ins on inquiries & bookings)
- Track everything in a shared spreadsheet (transparency reduces disputes)
